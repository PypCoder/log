# Vector Spaces & Cosine Similarity

LLMs do not process raw text directly; instead, they map semantic concepts onto a high-dimensional geometric grid called a **vector space**. Modern embedding models typically utilize 1,500+ latent dimensions to capture intricate structural nuances such as tense, sentiment, and context.

## The Length Trap: Why Euclidean Distance Fails

When determining text similarity, standard Euclidean distance falls short due to document length distortion. For example, a short sentence and that exact same sentence repeated 50 times share the same core semantic meaning. However, the repetition spikes coordinate values far out into the high-dimensional grid. A straight-line distance check makes these conceptually identical texts appear completely unrelated.

## The Fix: Angular Direction

Instead of measuring the physical distance between spatial endpoints, we track the angle ($\theta$) of the vectors relative to the origin $(0,0,0)$. This mathematical shift completely isolates semantic direction from raw word count.

### 1. Dot Product
Measures the directional alignment of vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ across all dimensions:

$$
\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} A_i B_i
$$

### 2. Vector Magnitude
Calculates the absolute geometric length of an individual vector using the Euclidean norm:

$$
\|\vec{A}\| = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} A_i^2
}$$

### 3. Cosine Similarity Ratio
Strips away the scale/length of the documents, isolating the pure angular metric:

$$
\cos(\theta) = \frac{\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}}{\|\vec{A}\| \|\vec{B}\|}
$$

## Metric Interpretation

*   **1 ($0^\circ$):** Vectors point in the exact same direction (identical semantic meaning).
*   **0 ($90^\circ$):** Vectors are perpendicular (completely independent concepts).
*   **-1 ($180^\circ$):** Vectors point in opposite directions (direct semantic opposites).
